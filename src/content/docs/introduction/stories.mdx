## Our Context

We are CCGCM (the Collaborative Centre for Genomic Cancer Medicine) - a centre
formed from genomic cancer researchers from Peter Mac
and University of Melbourne.

We do a mix of clinical and research genomic sequencing - with a variety
of assays. We also do novel cancer research, both within the core
team of the centre and in associated research groups.

## Our Stories

Our work on GUARDIANS data sharing is motivated by solving
underlying stories we encounter each day. Not all our stories
are national focussed - some can be solved by a simple mouse click in
existing software. We start though with an enumeration of our sharing
stories to provide some context for all of our work. To be clear,
not all of these stories are relevant to our work in GUARDIANS - but
we felt presenting them all up front would give a clear picture
of where our sharing solutions come from.

For each story we have given some "distinguishing features" that
we have used in our thinking about solutions for these stories.

### Story "Sequencer software sharing"

> "I am a collaborator with the centre and we have our own
> account in the software environment provided
> by your sequencing machine vendor.
> I would like some sequencing data or pipeline runs from
> the CCGCM sequencer copied or shared directly into my account."

**Distinguishing features**

- Vendor authorisation story
- Sequencer machine as a back up for others

### Story "Adhoc send it to my bucket sharing"

> "I am a collaborator within an external institution that
> sends samples to CCGCM for sequencing and clinical curation reporting.
> We would like a copy of the analysis data that
> is generated from the curation reporting as part
> of our research governance - and to this end
> our IT department have made us a bucket appropriate
> for depositing data. We only occasionally ask for this data so there
> is no standing agreement for this process - we just email
> our contact at CCGCM."

**Distinguishing features**

- Copy out
- Specific destination
- Adhoc

{/*Tech keywords
data-sharing-manager, S3
Solution sketch
s3-steps-role is given write access to S3 bucket. Use data-sharing-manager to copy all data over to the collaborators S3 bucket.*/}

### Story "Routine send it to my bucket sharing"

> "I am a postdoctoral researcher working at CCGCM in a research group. We sequence
> samples regularly, and I track sequencing runs and request the corresponding
> FASTQ files when I expect the results to be available. Our group manages a dozen
> ongoing projects; when data is requested, the Genomics Platform Team pushes
> it to a location we can access in our own research environments."

**Distinguishing features**

- Copy out
- Metadata lookup
- Specific destination
- Automatable

{/*Tech keywords
data sharing manager, S3, steps-s3-copy, GitHub
Solution sketch
A data-sharing agreement stored in a GitHub repository defines the terms
 of data sharing, including project identifiers, data ownership, contact
  recipients and the S3 destinations where the data will be pushed. Upon
   completion of a sequencing run analysis, an automated tool identifies
    all data that match the agreement. Data recipients raise GitHub
     issues with the requested library details; the Genomics Platform
      Team validates the request against the identified data,
       delivers FASTQ files to the defined S3 path, and the requester
        verifies and closes the issue, creating an auditable
         record of each data share.*/}

### Story "Download it myself"

> "I am a researcher at an external university. I do  not have any cloud experience
> and would prefer to do my research on a HPC. Each month CCGCM generates
> some data for me and I would like to be given some
> scripts so that we can download our data onto our HPC."

**Distinguishing features**

- Download
- Egress cost considerations

{/*
Tech keywords
Presigned urls, HPC, zero-cloud

Solution sketch
The datasharing service uses the filemanager to generate a presigned url for all files. 
When a presign request comes in, the API collects all presigned urls for all files in the request and then a lambda generates a shell script that is written to s3. 
A presigned url is then generated of that script which can be shared with the client.
*/}

### Story "Internal use of clinical data"

> "I am a bioinformatician working on some methods development
> at CCGCM. I am employed through CCGCM and have familiarity
> with the cloud. CCGCM has sequenced a patient
> that has exhibited some edge cases I want to test with my software.

> This is patient data so I would like to do some work in a privileged
> account (i.e. not a common developers account).

> I just want to be left alone with my cloud account and some 
> ways to read the data from the account - so I can do work using native cloud
> tools.

> After my work is done I promise I will try to remember to let
> someone know and the access to the data can be removed - though it would
> be good if there were other mechanisms to notice these shares as I may forget.

**Distinguishing features**

- Zero copy
- Cloud engineer/bioinformatics power user
- Patient data so not in dev
- Internal query for discovery

{/*Tech keywords
Access points, CloudFormation, S3

Solution sketch
Create some form of mart(?) query to identify the S3 URLs I want to access
Ask someone to install something in the data control account that enables sharing of the objects to the account I have access to

Parts of the story dealt with elsewhere
Getting access to an AWS account that is appropriate (one for centre management and policy)
Lifecycle management (assume the objects are live or been restored through some other mechanism?)*/}


### Story "Researcher environment"

> "I am a new PhD researcher working for a research group at CCGCM and am
> employed by Peter Mac. I have lots of bioinformatics experience but
> very little cloud compute. It has been decided I will be given access
> to a small subset of project data for my initial project work and 
> that I will be writing the workflows in a workflow language like Nextflow. I am
> only going to be allowed to access the variant data from the
> participants. I want to scalably run pipelines over the data.

**Distinguishing features**

- Zero copy
- Research environment is ours to lock down as we see fit
- Costs and bills come to us - to apportion if we want
- Cloud newbie

htsget-rs, VPC Lattice, Seqera, terraform

Solution sketch
Someone within centre uses a tool to take known identifiers from researcher and constructs VCF list.
htsget is configured with a config file (?) that authorises this share of variants across into the designated account via htsget vpc lattice

Parts of the story dealt with elsewhere
Lifecycle management (assume the objects are live or been restored through some other mechanism?)*/}


### Story "External researcher environment"

> "I am a researcher at an institute who wants to perform some research on a large established CCGCM cohort. 
> My IT department has created a locked down trusted researcher environment - in the same cloud as CCGCM - though
> under the control of my institute. I would like to apply for a subset of the cohort
> as aligned reads and have access to them in my environment. I would like access
> to the data for one month."

**Distinguishing features**

- Research environment is under control of different organisation
- Time limits on the data access
- Cohort subset
- Large-scale cohort with large data
