## Overarching User Story

As a researcher, I want to be to do my research of human genomics data in a safe environment that allows me to
meet my ethical obligations to participants.

As a data custodian, I want to be able to provide access to the data under my custodianship in a safe environment
that allows me to meet my ethical obligations to participants.

Whilst both of these user stories acknowledge the ethical obligations to keep the data of
participants safe and controlled – there is an inherent tension between the two. The researcher wants
to do their research with as few restrictions as possible. On the other hand, the data custodian
wants to allow research but coming from the perspective of minimising the risks.

See SATRE Architectural Principle 1.


## SATRE

Rather than reinvent the wheel for the requirements of genomics research environments, we choose
instead to leverage the excellent work of HDR-UK and the SATRE project who have already
done extensive requirements analysis and solution design for researcher environments in the UK.

[SATRE 2.0.1](https://satre-specification.readthedocs.io/en/v2.0.1/) (pinned as the version that was evaluated against)

Our path then is not to invent new requirements in isolation - but instead to use SATRE as a
point of reference. Do the needs of a genomics researcher *add* substantially
different requirements to the SATRE story? Are there some requirements that we would
constrast as being unnecessary or indeed detrimental for genomics research? Can we add
roles that clarify the way data custodians and researchers behave in the human genomics community? 

### Architectural Principles

SATRE starts with Architectural Principles. We can see no reason why genomics would
change these principles - all are sound principles that equally apply to genomics research. Nor can we
think of any principles that would need to be added to correctly underpin genomics research.

For reference, we repeat the four principles here.

* Usability - A TRE instance that works for all users minimises barriers to use, and provides
  a productive and accessible analysis environment for research.
* Retaining public trust - TREs holding public data should build and maintain the trust
  of data subjects and any other impacted individuals, groups, communities and organisations 
  by protecting privacy, keeping data secure and being transparent about their work.
* Observability - Human initiated and automated processes resulting in change within the
  TRE should be observable.
* Standardisation - TREs should adhere to standards or well-known patterns wherever possible.

### Pillars

1. Information Governance
2. Computing Technology
3. Data Management
4. Supporting Capabilities

Again, there is little reason to think that genomics would fundamentally change these pillars.
