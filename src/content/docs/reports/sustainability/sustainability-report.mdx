## Keeping the nodes running

Building research infrastructure is a project. Keeping it running is a commitment. Grants fund the first, and rarely the second.

That gap is what this sustainability plan sets out to close. GUARDIANS Phase I gave CCGCM and its partners tools for genomic data nodes, and most are already in production. Whether they stay maintained, usable and open to researchers across Australia depends on funding and ownership that no single project grant can provide.

In the pages that follow, we look at what Phase I leaves us, where the current arrangement runs out, and how other national programmes have solved the same problem. We then propose a funding model built around a national operator, and set out the Phase II actions and risks that come with it.

## From Phase I to Phase II: outputs and continuing components

Much of our Phase I work carries over. The genomic data node concept still holds, 
and many of the tools for moving, checking and granting access to data will be used 
directly in Phase II. CCGCM plans to use the hardened Seqera platform as a research environment, 
and as a step towards a TRE, so it will keep maintaining these tools. That is not an institutional 
guarantee of long-term sustainability. Some exploratory work will not be taken 
forward: **curtrail** (replaced by AWS CloudWatch Logs in Parquet format), 
**orcabus-elsa-data** and our IPv6 exploration.

The components carried into Phase II are:

- **htsget-rs.** Production server for streaming regions of genomic files (BAM, CRAM, VCF) without copying whole datasets. In production at CCGCM and other organisations.
- **REMS Terraform Provider.** Manages REMS data access forms, workflows, licences and catalogue items as code. Production grade, with no alternative available.
- **Seqera setup with Terraform.** Creates paired AWS accounts and Seqera workspaces on the BioCommons Seqera instance. Phase II will use it to provision research environments.
- **AAF integration.** Institutional login through the Australian Access Federation, central to our production authentication.
- **VPC Lattice.** A production-grade way to connect AWS accounts securely, and our intended approach for AWS networking in Phase II.
- **steps-s3-copy (with lifecycle support).** Large-scale, verified copying into the data node, including restore from cold storage and pre-run cost estimates. Kept because it meets specialist requirements that similar AWS, Cloudflare and Google services do not, and costs less to run.
- **copyrite.** Checksumming and copying with verified integrity. AWS and Google now offer whole-object checksums natively, which covers some of its use cases, but it remains part of our Phase II copy tooling.



## Maintenance, funding and open questions

### Ongoing ownership, stewardship, hosting or maintenance arrangements

All software developed during Phase I is open source, and we welcome contributions and change requests from the community. Like most academic open-source software, however, it has no formal institutional backing. Its development has been grant funded, and without sustained community uptake or long-term funding, individual tools risk becoming unmaintained over time.

Several of these components are also used in CCGCM's own production laboratory work, irrespective of national data sharing. steps-s3-copy is used to move data, and AAF handles production portal logins for the centre. htsget is a possible way CCGCM would do production IGV in the future. Designing components for use in both the production lab side and national data sharing ensures there is at least an economic motivation to keep them sustained from the ongoing lab work.

### Key resourcing, funding, infrastructure or partnership dependencies

Continued coordination with Australian BioCommons on the Seqera tooling will be essential, given the platform's role as a shared research environment. We also intend to reuse these components, and fund further development, through current and future grants for Trusted Research Environments (TREs).

### Unresolved sustainability matters to be addressed in Phase II

We will seek alignment with Genomics Australia and with MRFF funding calls for TREs, including federated TREs. Long-term sustainability will nonetheless remain a risk until NCRIS, Bioplatforms Australia or a comparable body recognises genomic research environments as critical national infrastructure. That recognition would need to come with ongoing funding for an organisation able to take on their maintenance and development. The issue will become more pressing as TREs mature and incur recurring operational costs, including help desk support, continuous security monitoring and day-to-day operations.

### Significant changes from the Phase II sustainability approach

We have raised these concerns at earlier progress meetings and workshops, and no clear solution has yet emerged. We regard this as a systemic issue: national infrastructure cannot be sustainably maintained or operated through project-based grant funding. Individual GUARDIANS partners have neither the incentive nor the legal remit to operate computational infrastructure for external users, let alone at national scale. We therefore strongly recommend planning a transition process with Bioplatforms Australia, NCRIS and other relevant stakeholders throughout Phase II.
