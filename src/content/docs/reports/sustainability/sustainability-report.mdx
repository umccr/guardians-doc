Building research infrastructure is a project. Keeping it running is a commitment. Grants fund the first, and rarely the second.

That gap is what this sustainability plan sets out to close. GUARDIANS Phase I gave CCGCM and its partners tools for genomic data nodes, and most are already in production. Whether they stay maintained, usable and open to researchers across Australia depends on funding and ownership that no single project grant can provide.

In the pages that follow, we look at what Phase I leaves us, where the current arrangement runs out, and how other national programmes have solved the same problem. We then propose a funding model built around a national operator, and set out the Phase II actions and risks that come with it.

## From Phase I to Phase II: outputs and continuing components

Much of our Phase I work carries over. The genomic data node concept still holds, 
and many of the tools for moving, checking and granting access to data will be used 
directly in Phase II. CCGCM plans to use the hardened Seqera platform as a research environment, 
and as a step towards a TRE, so it will keep maintaining these tools. That is not an institutional 
guarantee of long-term sustainability. Some exploratory work will not be taken 
forward: **curtrail** (replaced by AWS CloudWatch Logs in Parquet format), 
**orcabus-elsa-data** and our IPv6 exploration.

The components carried into Phase II are:

- **htsget-rs.** Production server for streaming regions of genomic files (BAM, CRAM, VCF) without copying whole datasets. In production at CCGCM and other organisations.
- **REMS Terraform Provider.** Manages REMS data access forms, workflows, licences and catalogue items as code. Production grade, with no alternative available.
- **Seqera setup with Terraform.** Creates paired AWS accounts and Seqera workspaces on the BioCommons Seqera instance. Phase II will use it to provision research environments.
- **AAF integration.** Institutional login through the Australian Access Federation, central to our production authentication.
- **VPC Lattice.** A production-grade way to connect AWS accounts securely, and our intended approach for AWS networking in Phase II.
- **steps-s3-copy (with lifecycle support).** Large-scale, verified copying into the data node, including restore from cold storage and pre-run cost estimates. Kept because it meets specialist requirements that similar AWS, Cloudflare and Google services do not, and costs less to run.
- **copyrite.** Checksumming and copying with verified integrity. AWS and Google now offer whole-object checksums natively, which covers some of its use cases, but it remains part of our Phase II copy tooling.



## Maintenance, funding and open questions

### Ongoing ownership, stewardship, hosting or maintenance arrangements

All software developed during Phase I is open source, and we welcome contributions and change requests from the community. Like most academic open-source software, however, it has no formal institutional backing. Its development has been grant funded, and without sustained community uptake or long-term funding, individual tools risk becoming unmaintained over time.

Several of these components are also used in CCGCM's own production laboratory work, irrespective of national data sharing. steps-s3-copy is used to move data, and AAF handles production portal logins for the centre. htsget is a possible way CCGCM would do production IGV in the future. Designing components for use in both the production lab side and national data sharing ensures there is at least an economic motivation to keep them sustained from the ongoing lab work.

### Key resourcing, funding, infrastructure or partnership dependencies

Continued coordination with Australian BioCommons on the Seqera tooling will be essential, given the platform's role as a shared research environment. We also intend to reuse these components, and fund further development, through current and future grants for Trusted Research Environments (TREs).

### Unresolved sustainability matters to be addressed in Phase II

We will seek alignment with Genomics Australia and with MRFF funding calls for TREs, including federated TREs. Long-term sustainability will nonetheless remain a risk until NCRIS, Bioplatforms Australia or a comparable body recognises genomic research environments as critical national infrastructure. That recognition would need to come with ongoing funding for an organisation able to take on their maintenance and development. The issue will become more pressing as TREs mature and incur recurring operational costs, including help desk support, continuous security monitoring and day-to-day operations.

### Significant changes from the Phase II sustainability approach

We have raised these concerns at earlier progress meetings and workshops, and no clear solution has yet emerged. We regard this as a systemic issue: national infrastructure cannot be sustainably maintained or operated through project-based grant funding. Individual GUARDIANS partners have neither the incentive nor the legal remit to operate computational infrastructure for external users, let alone at national scale. We therefore strongly recommend planning a transition process with Bioplatforms Australia, NCRIS and other relevant stakeholders throughout Phase II.


## Where Phase I leaves us

Phase I has produced a working set of genomic data node capabilities: secure data copying and staging, region-level data access with htsget, access management with REMS, AAF-based authentication, cross-account AWS networking, and initial exploration of the Seqera Platform as a research environment. Most are already in production at CCGCM, and htsget-rs is also used by other organisations. A small number of exploratory outputs (curtrail, orcabus-elsa-data and the IPv6 exploration) will not continue, but their findings have informed the Phase II design.

All CCGCM software is open source and accepts community contributions. CCGCM will keep maintaining the set of components listed above as the organisation plans to use a hardened Seqera platform as its own research environment. This resourcing approach comes with limitations:

- **Grant-funded development.** Maintenance depends on future project funding.
- **No national mandate.** Beyond the GUARDIANS grant, current participants are not funded to maintain the software or to support its use by external partners, let alone at national scale.
- **Rising operating costs.** As research environments mature towards TREs, they need help desk support, continuous security monitoring and day-to-day operations. These are recurring staff costs that project grants are not designed to cover.

As part of the overall sustainability planning, we do recommend that GUARDIANS should be positioned as a funded research infrastructure pilot, as recommended by the National Approach to Genomic Information Management (NAGIM). We recommend that Phase II include a planned transition to a national operator, such as Genomics Australia or any other NCRIS-funded organisation, with a public base budget and a defined share of operating costs recovered from data submitters and users.


## What comparable programmes show

Genomics infrastructure is sustained where a national health entity or independent organisation owns it as part of clinical service delivery. Where it relies on research grants, even large initiatives face funding cliffs:

| Initiative | How it is funded and run |
| --- | --- |
| Genomics England (UK) | Company wholly owned by the Department of Health and Social Care; delivers sequencing with the NHS and runs the National Genomic Research Library |
| Genomic Medicine Sweden | Collaboration of seven healthcare regions and seven universities; annual government allocations. |
| PRECISE (Singapore) | Funding over five years from the Ministry of Health; Phase III works through the public healthcare clusters |
| All of Us (US) | Relied on time-limited legislative funding; budget reported down 72% since 2023 |
| AnVIL (US) | Renewed at a flat US$8m a year for 2028–2032, on condition of sharing costs between submitters, users and NIH |

In Australia, the 2023 NAGIM recommendations call for a funded pilot of research infrastructure, a national coordinating entity and an independent funding strategy, but provide no costings. The 2025 Clinical NAGIM report notes that national investment and progress have been limited, and directs implementation through Genomics Australia.

## Proposed sustainability model

We propose that a national entity, such as Genomics Australia, become the long-term operator of genomic data node infrastructure. Its funding would combine a public base budget with cost recovery for activities that scale with use, where each cost activity can be matched to a funding source best suited to support it. We outline a starting point for further discussions:

| Cost activity | Proposed funding source |
| --- | --- |
| Core operations: hosting, security monitoring, help desk, governance | Public base funding (NCRIS, Bioplatforms Australia or Genomics Australia) |
| Software maintenance and new development | Public base funding for maintenance and keeping up with international developments; research grants and commercial partnerships for more experimental developments |
| Data onboarding and storage | Federal funding as a key research resource |
| Analysis compute and data transfer | Users, through their own cloud accounts or research environments as funded by grants and other research funding sources |
| Shared research environment | Coordination with Australian BioCommons (Seqera) or commercial partner |

The base budget should be sized so that core data access stays free at the point of use. User fees for access would exclude researchers with limited resources and work against equitable use of national datasets. Cost recovery should therefore apply to activities that grow with use (storage, compute and transfer), not to access itself. The [Phase I costing report](../costing-data-sharing-platform/costing-scrollytell/) provides a starting point for estimating the standing costs of each element.

## Phase II: actions for the transition

We recommend that the transition be planned from the start of Phase II, not left to its conclusion:

1. **Agree the transition objective.** Confirm with the Australian BioCommons, Bioplatforms Australia, NCRIS and Genomics Australia that national operation is the intended end state, and who will organise the discussion.
2. **Cost the operating model.** Extend the Phase I costing report into standing-cost and per-use estimates for a national node.
3. **Prepare components for handover.** Keep documentation, infrastructure-as-code and security practices at a standard another operator can adopt without CCGCM staff.
4. **Pilot cost-sharing.** Use the Cancer of Unknown Primary (CUP) dataset sharing and Seqera workspaces to test submitter and user contributions in practice.
5. **Agree a handover point.** By the end of Phase II, either a national operator takes responsibility or the partners agree which components move to community maintenance.


## Risks

Finally, the transition set out above is not without risk:

- **No national operator emerges.** Without a decision by NCRIS, Bioplatforms Australia or Genomics Australia, the infrastructure remains dependent on grants and on CCGCM's own priorities.
- **Cost recovery is insufficient.** Submitter and user contributions cannot cover fixed operating costs; a base budget remains essential.
- **Cloud provider changes.** Providers change services and pricing, as with AWS deprecating CloudTrail Lake during Phase I. Tooling must be kept current.
