Data movement cost is what you pay to move data between users, projects, and organisations. It has two main components:

- **Platform infrastructure cost** – the services that enable data movement (APIs, orchestration, access control, logging).
- **Egress cost** – the cost of moving the bytes themselves (per GB), which varies depending on direction and distance.

Unlike storage cost (which grows gradually with data volume), data movement cost can spike when many users access data in a short period, or when large datasets are shared across regions or with external partners.

## Main cost components

At a high level, most data movement cost in a data‑sharing platform comes from a small set of building blocks:


### Platform infrastructure

To support data sharing, the platform must provide services that allow users and systems to discover, request and access data:

- **APIs and web services** that expose datasets and handle access requests.
- **Orchestration services** (e.g. AWS Step Functions, Lambda) that coordinate copy operations and approvals.
- **Access control and audit logging** to track who accessed what, when and under what policy.
- Optional **caching or gateway layers** to optimise repeated access to popular datasets.

These services have:

- A **baseline standing cost** (covered in the standing‑cost document), and  
- A **usage‑dependent component** that grows with the number of requests, transfers and jobs.

In most platforms, infrastructure cost is modest compared to egress, unless request volumes or logging detail are very high.

AWS pricing references:  
[API Gateway](https://aws.amazon.com/api-gateway/pricing/), [Lambda](https://aws.amazon.com/lambda/pricing/), [Step Functions](https://aws.amazon.com/step-functions/pricing/), [CloudWatch](https://aws.amazon.com/cloudwatch/pricing/).


### Internal transfers (same account, same region)

When data moves **within the same AWS account and region**:

- Reads from S3 to compute, or writes from compute back to S3, are typically free or very cheap from a data transfer perspective.
- Internal S3 copies (between buckets in the same region) generally incur no data transfer egress charges, only S3 request charges.

These internal movements:

- Drive some infrastructure usage (APIs, orchestration, logging).
- Rarely dominate the bill; they are usually dwarfed by external egress if present.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/), [EC2 data transfer](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/).


### Cross‑account and cross‑region transfers

Data‑sharing platforms often need to move data:

- **Across accounts** – for sharing between teams or organisations in the same region.
- **Across regions** – for resilience, compliance or performance.

Cross‑account transfers within the same region:

- Can often be implemented with bucket policies or VPC endpoints at negligible egress cost.

Cross‑region transfers:

- Incur per‑GB charges for data moved between regions.
- Matter most when replicating large datasets or frequently syncing caches.

These cross‑region movements are often the first clear source of non‑trivial data movement cost even before external egress.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3 replication and multi‑region storage](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/).


### External egress (out of AWS)

When data leaves the AWS region to external destinations:

- **To the public internet** – for example users downloading to local machines – incurs per‑GB data transfer charges.
- **To on‑premises environments** – via VPN or Direct Connect – also incurs per‑GB charges (often with a different pricing profile).
- **To other cloud providers** – generally treated as internet egress from AWS.

External egress is typically the **most expensive** form of data movement on a per‑GB basis. For CSPs, external egress can be the dominant movement cost.

AWS pricing references:  
[EC2 / S3 data transfer out](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/), [Direct Connect](https://aws.amazon.com/directconnect/pricing/).


### Caching and reuse

Platforms can reduce repeated data movement by:

- **Caching commonly accessed datasets** closer to consumers (e.g. regional cache buckets).
- Encouraging **reuse of shared copies** instead of creating independent copies per user.

A good caching strategy:

- Reduces repeated external egress.
- Reduces the need for repeated cross‑region transfers.
- Shifts some cost from movement to storage (cache buckets), which is often cheaper and more predictable.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/), [CloudFront](https://aws.amazon.com/cloudfront/pricing/) (if used for caching/distribution).


## Internal vs cross‑organisation platforms: different movement patterns

The same main components appear in both an **internal sharing platform (ISP)** and a **cross‑organisation sharing platform (CSP)**, but the way data moves is different.

### Internal Sharing Platform

An ISP shares data **within a single organisation**, typically within one AWS region:

- Users and projects live in the same account or a small set of linked accounts.
- Most data movement is internal (same region), with negligible egress cost.
- Sharing is often “pull” – users fetch data from central buckets – rather than “push” – platform copying data out to other clouds.
- Audit logging is tuned to internal compliance requirements.

**Typical movement pattern**:

- Tens of projects, each accessing shared datasets multiple times per month.
- Per access, data volumes may be tens of GB, but mostly internal.
- Cross‑region movement may be limited to resilience uses (replication to a secondary region).

**Data movement cost shape**:

- Infrastructure cost (APIs, orchestration, logging) is non‑zero but modest.
- Internal movement cost is very low.
- Cross‑region cost is only significant where resilience or multi‑region operation is required.
- External egress is low or negligible in many ISP scenarios.


### Cross‑Organisation Sharing Platform

A CSP regularly shares data with **external partners**, often across regions and organisations:

- Partners are in different AWS accounts, different organisations, and sometimes different regions.
- Sharing may be “push” (platform copies data into partner accounts or delivers via managed transfers) as well as “pull”.
- Data may need to be replicated to partner regions, or delivered to on‑premises or other clouds.
- Audit logging is typically more detailed and retention longer to meet cross‑organisational governance expectations.

**Typical movement pattern**:

- Internal projects accessing datasets, plus external partners pulling or receiving copies.
- Cross‑account and cross‑region transfers for partner access and resilience.
- External egress to on‑premises or other clouds when partners download locally.

**Data movement cost shape**:

- Infrastructure cost grows with the number of partners, endpoints and policies.
- Cross‑region movement cost grows with the number of replicated datasets and regions.
- External egress often becomes the dominant movement cost, especially when partners download large datasets.
- Caching and reuse become important levers for controlling movement cost.


## Design choices

Several design decisions strongly affect data movement cost:

### Custom service vs AWS primitives

Platforms can implement data sharing using:

**Custom data‑movement service**  

- Example: a service using Lambda and Step Functions to coordinate S3 copies between accounts.
- Pros: tailored to the platform’s needs; can optimise movement patterns and caching for cost.
- Cons: requires development and ongoing maintenance; needs careful design for scale and robustness.

**AWS primitives and managed services**  

- Examples: S3 cross‑account access, S3 Batch Operations, AWS DataSync, Transfer Family.
- Pros: managed services with lower operational burden; built‑in scalability and reliability.
- Cons: less tailored; some managed services may have higher per‑GB or per‑operation cost in specific workloads.

In many cases, a **lightweight custom service** built on core AWS primitives strikes a good balance, but part of this costing exercise is to understand if and when managed alternatives might be cheaper or simpler.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/), [S3 Batch Operations](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/), [DataSync](https://aws.amazon.com/datasync/pricing/), [Transfer Family](https://aws.amazon.com/aws-transfer-family/pricing/).


### Internal vs cross‑account vs external movement

The **path data takes** matters for cost:

- **Internal, same account and region** – generally free or very low cost (ignoring minimal request charges).
- **Cross‑account, same region** – often achievable with bucket policies and endpoints at negligible egress cost.
- **Cross‑region** – per‑GB charges that grow with replicated datasets and sync frequency.
- **External** – internet or Direct Connect egress charges per GB, often the largest per‑GB cost.

Design implications:

- To minimise data movement cost, keep most sharing **within one region** and use cross‑account access where possible.
- Use cross‑region replication only where resilience, performance or compliance demands it.
- Manage and monitor external egress carefully, as it can grow quickly with partner usage.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/), [EC2 data transfer](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/), [Direct Connect](https://aws.amazon.com/directconnect/pricing/).


### Caching vs copying

Another major lever is how repeated access is handled:

**Copying**  

- Each user or partner receives their own full copy of datasets.
- Pros: simple mental model; each consumer controls their own copy.
- Cons: multiplies storage cost and increases egress; 10 partners copying 100 GB each produces 1 TB of extra storage and 1 TB of extra movement.

**Caching and shared copies**  

- Frequently accessed datasets are cached closer to consumers, or shared via controlled access to a central bucket.
- Pros: avoids redundant copies; reduces repeated egress and cross‑region movement.
- Cons: requires cache design and management; policies must ensure correct access control.

For a data‑sharing platform, **caching and reuse** are usually preferable to unconstrained copying, both for cost and for coherence of shared datasets.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/), [CloudFront](https://aws.amazon.com/cloudfront/pricing/).


### Region and pathway strategy

Region choice and routing strategy influence movement cost:

- Keeping most data in one region minimises cross‑region cost.
- Choosing which region acts as the hub for external egress changes per‑GB pricing.
- Using private pathways (e.g. VPC endpoints, peering) can reduce unintended cross‑region or internet movement.

Thoughtful region and pathway design can avoid hidden movement costs and make expected movement easier to monitor.

AWS pricing references:  
[VPC endpoints / PrivateLink](https://aws.amazon.com/privatelink/pricing/), [VPC peering](https://aws.amazon.com/vpc/pricing/), [EC2 data transfer](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/).

### Assumptions and estimates

Below, we describe representative setups we assumed for an ISP and a CSP, and the corresponding estimates for each component.

#### Platform infrastructure (APIs, orchestration, logging)

**ISP**

- Core API endpoints for internal users.  
- Moderate usage of Lambda, Step Functions and orchestration jobs.  
- CloudWatch logging tuned to internal needs (metrics and logs retained for months, not years).  
- Typical monthly cost: **\$50–150** for these shared services.

**CSP**

- Additional APIs for partner access (e.g. external entrypoints, more complex routing).  
- Higher Lambda and Step Functions usage due to partner workflows and policy checks.  
- More detailed logging and longer retention to support cross‑organisational audit.  
- Typical monthly cost: **\$150–300**, reflecting broader use and higher logging needs.

AWS pricing references:  
[API Gateway](https://aws.amazon.com/api-gateway/pricing/), [Lambda](https://aws.amazon.com/lambda/pricing/), [Step Functions](https://aws.amazon.com/step-functions/pricing/), [CloudWatch](https://aws.amazon.com/cloudwatch/pricing/).


#### Internal movement (same region)

**ISP**

- Datasets typically accessed by internal projects and analysis jobs within the same region.  
- Movement is mostly reads from S3 to compute and writes back, with negligible egress cost.  
- Occasional reorganisation or intra‑bucket copies.  
- Typical monthly cost attributable to movement: **\$0–20**, primarily request and minor log costs.

**CSP**

- Similar internal usage within each account, plus some internal cross‑account movement via bucket policies or VPC endpoints.  
- Internal movement costs remain low, but logging and orchestration around these accesses add small overhead.  
- Typical monthly cost: **\$20–50**, including logging and request charges.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/), [EC2 data transfer](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/).


#### Cross‑region movement

**ISP**

- Some platforms replicate key datasets to a secondary region for resilience.  
- Typical pattern: copying new or changed datasets, TB‑scale per month at most.  
- Typical monthly cost: **\$0–50**, depending on how much data is replicated and how often.

**CSP**

- CSPs may replicate data for partners in multiple regions, or for global resilience and performance.  
- Movement may be tens of GB to a few TB per month across regions.  
- Typical monthly cost: **\$50–150**, depending on the volume of cross‑region transfers.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3 replication and multi‑region storage](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/).


#### External egress (internet / on‑premises)

**ISP**

- Internal sharing often involves little or no external egress; analysts work within the platform or internal environments.  
- Occasional external download of smaller datasets for local work.  
- Typical monthly cost: **\$0–50** for external egress.

**CSP**

- Regular downloads by partners to on‑premises or other clouds, often GB‑ to TB‑scale per month.  
- Egress to the internet or via Direct Connect becomes a significant line item.  
- Typical monthly cost: **\$100–300**, depending on partner behaviour and dataset sizes.

AWS pricing references:  
[EC2 / S3 data transfer out](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/), [Direct Connect](https://aws.amazon.com/directconnect/pricing/).
