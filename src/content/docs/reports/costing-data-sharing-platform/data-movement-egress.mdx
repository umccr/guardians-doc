Data movement cost is what you pay to move data around. In the data-sharing platform we are costing, analysis is already done, 
so data movement is about getting finished data to teams, external partners, and different storage tiers—not shuffling it between analysis stages.

We can consider data movement as two main contributions:


- **Platform infrastructure cost** – the services that enable data movement (e.g. custom orchestration stacks, APIs, access control, logging,
 or intermediate storage for data staging).

- **Egress cost** – the cost imposed by the provider for moving the bytes themselves (per GB). This varies depending on where data moves: 
within a region, across regions, or out to the internet.

In general, there is a key asymmetry: **uploading data into AWS is free; moving data out costs money.** This asymmetry applies not just 
to external egress, but also to movement between storage tiers, where moving data out of a lower-tier storage class can incur significant charges 
(see [Looking after data](../data-lifecycle/) for details).

Unlike storage cost (which grows gradually with data volume), data movement cost is **event-driven**: sudden spikes occur when datasets are 
shared with new partners, replicated to additional regions, moved between storage tiers, or accessed at scale.

## Main cost components

To understand where data movement costs come from, it helps to follow the journey of a dataset: first, the infrastructure that orchestrates its movement, 
then the actual cost of moving the bytes themselves—which varies dramatically depending on where they go.

### Platform infrastructure

The services that orchestrate data movement incur both standing and usage-dependent costs:

- **Orchestration services** coordinate copy jobs, handle approvals, and manage workflows. This can be custom-built using Step Functions and Lambda 
(charging per state transition and invocation), or use AWS managed services like 
[DataSync](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/datasync/latest/userguide/what-is-datasync.html). For cross-institutional or federated data sharing, 
platforms like [Globus](https://www.globus.org/) provide managed orchestration and transfer services with their own pricing models. 
The orchestration choice significantly affects this cost component and is discussed in [Design choices](#design-choices).


- **APIs and access control** expose datasets and enforce permissions. Beyond the baseline standing cost, API Gateway charges per request, 
which can become significant for high-volume access patterns. While IAM policy evaluation itself is free (cost of high access is not expected here), 
audit logging (CloudWatch Logs, CloudTrail) tracks who accessed what, when, and under what policy; charges scale with event volume and retention period.


- **Intermediate storage** (working buckets, staging areas) holds temporary artifacts during sharing orchestration—job manifests, 
thaw requests, copy instructions—and working buckets needed for data operations: S3 buckets for staging data before moving, 
or hot buckets for thawing objects from cold storage tiers before transfer. While artifact buckets are normally small, they are persistent and generate request charges from frequent reads/writes during orchestration. 
Staging buckets, by contrast, are ephemeral—created and deleted as needed—but can accumulate significant costs if data movement volume is high.


These services incur both standing and usage-dependent costs. The baseline cost is covered in [Standing costs](../standing-costs/);
 the usage-dependent component grows with the number of copy jobs, API requests, audit events, and data movement volume. In most platforms, 
 infrastructure cost is modest compared to egress, unless request volumes, logging detail, or staging activity are very high.


### Egress cost: where bytes travel

The cost of moving bytes depends entirely on where data goes. AWS charges per-GB rates that vary by pathway, from free (internal) 
to expensive (external). Understanding each pathway helps you anticipate costs and design accordingly.

- **Internal transfers (same account, same AWS region)**: Reads from S3 to compute, writes back to S3, and internal S3 copies all 
incur no data transfer charges—only S3 request charges. Note that moving data between storage classes within the same region 
(e.g. retrieving archived data from Glacier before sharing) also incurs no egress charges, but can have associated retrieval and request fees. 
These internal movements rarely dominate the bill.


- **Cross-account transfers (same AWS region)**: Cross-account transfers can bse implemented via bucket policies or VPC endpoints at negligible egress cost. 
This is significantly cheaper than moving data across regions.

- **Cross-region transfers**: Cross-region transfers incur per-GB charges (typically of the order of ~$0.02/GB, varying by region pair). These costs 
matter most when replicating large datasets for resilience, compliance, or performance. Cross-region transfers are often the first clear source of 
non-trivial data movement cost.

- **External egress (out of AWS)**: External egress is the most expensive pathway. Data leaving AWS to the public internet, on-premises environments 
(via VPN or Direct Connect), or other cloud providers all incur per-GB charges. Internet egress typically costs $0.09–0.12/GB depending on region. 
External egress can easily becomes the dominant movement cost.



## Internal vs cross‑organisation platforms: different movement patterns

Data movement patterns differ significantly between ISP and CSP, shaping how costs accumulate over time.

### Internal Sharing Platform (ISP)

An ISP shares data **within a single organisation**, typically within one AWS region:

- Users and projects live in the same account or a small set of linked accounts.
- Most data movement is internal (same region), with negligible egress cost.
- Sharing is "pull" – users fetch data from central buckets – rather than "push" to external systems.
- Audit logging is tuned to internal compliance requirements.

**Typical movement pattern**:

- 50 active projects, each team accessing shared reference datasets and analysis results several times per month.
- Per access, data volumes are tens of GB, but movement is internal (negligible egress cost).
- Cross‑region replication may exist for resilience, but is not routine.

**Data movement cost shape**:

- Infrastructure cost is modest.
- Internal movement cost is negligible.
- Cross‑region cost appears only where resilience is required.
- External egress is low or absent.

### Cross‑Organisation Sharing Platform (CSP)

A CSP regularly shares data with **external partners**, often across regions and organisations:

- Partners are in different AWS accounts, different organisations, and sometimes different regions.
- Sharing is both "pull" (partners fetch from your buckets) and "push" (platform copies into partner accounts or systems).
- Data is often replicated across regions to meet data residency requirements or provide low-latency access.
- Audit logging is detailed and retention is long to meet cross‑organisational governance standards.

**Typical movement pattern**:

- 20 internal projects plus 10 external partners.
- Partners regularly request copies of datasets (100 GB to 1 TB per request), either downloaded to their systems or replicated into 
their AWS accounts in different regions.
- Some partners access data via APIs for federated queries.
- Replication to 2–3 regions is routine for compliance and resilience.

**Data movement cost shape**:

- Infrastructure cost grows with the number of partners, API endpoints, and policy complexity.
- Cross‑region movement cost grows steadily as datasets are replicated to multiple regions.
- External egress becomes the dominant cost driver, especially when partners download large datasets.
- Caching and strategic replication become critical levers for cost control.


## Design choices

Data movement cost is where design choices have the most immediate impact. A thoughtful architecture keeps costs predictable and 
controllable; poor choices can turn data movement into your largest and most volatile cost line item. Here are the key decisions that shape 
your cost trajectory.

### Custom orchestration vs AWS managed services

Platforms can implement data sharing orchestration using:

**Custom orchestration** (e.g. Step Functions and Lambda coordinating S3 copies)

- Pros: tailored to your data characteristics (large objects, cold storage retrieval, cross-account patterns); can optimise movement 
patterns and caching for cost.
- Cons: requires development and ongoing maintenance; needs careful design for scale and robustness.

**AWS managed services** (e.g. S3 Batch Operations, DataSync, Transfer Family)

- Pros: lower operational burden; built-in scalability and reliability.
- Cons: less tailored to specific workloads; have higher per-GB or per-operation costs.

A lightweight custom service built on core AWS primitives often strikes a good balance (see e.g. [Steps S3 Copy](/guardians-doc/technology/steps-s3-copy))
 However, understanding the cost trade-offs between custom and managed approaches is essential for your platform's long-term sustainability.
 

### Movement pathway: designing for cost

How you route data through your infrastructure is one of the most powerful levers for controlling cost. Keep data in one region where possible.
 Replicate selectively—only datasets that justify the cost, only to regions where partners operate. Encourage federated access via APIs rather than
  direct downloads. Active monitoring and governance of data pathways are essential to prevent unplanned replication and cost drift. The key is 
  intentionality: every copy, every region, every external download should serve a purpose.


### Sharing strategy: copying vs caching

How do you handle repeated access to the same dataset? 

**Full copying** – each partner receives their own complete copy

- Pros: simple; each partner has full control.
- Cons: multiplies storage and egress costs.

**Caching and shared access** – frequently accessed datasets are cached or accessed via controlled central buckets

- Pros: avoids redundant copies; reduces repeated egress and cross-region movement.
- Cons: requires cache design and management; access policies must be carefully maintained.

For most data-sharing platforms, **caching and reuse** are preferable to unconstrained copying, both for cost and for maintaining coherence of 
shared datasets. Consider which datasets justify full copies (e.g. long-term partner collaborations) versus which should be cached or accessed on-demand.


### Region strategy: hub vs distributed

Where you store data and where your partners access it from determines both latency and cost.

**Single region** – all data in one region, partners access remotely. 
- Pros: simple, cheap. 
- Cons: poor latency for distant partners.

**Multi-region reference data** – replicate reference datasets to a second region where partners operate. 
- Pros: fast local access, avoids repeated egress. 
-Cons: higher transfer cost.

**On-demand replication** – replicate project outputs only when partners request them.
- Pros: balances cost and flexibility. 
- Cons: requires coordination and delays.



## Data movement cost at a glance

Data movement cost is event-driven, not gradual. Unlike storage, which grows steadily with data volume,
 data movement spikes when you make architectural choices—replicate to a new region, let partners download freely, or scale to new collaborators. 
 This makes data movement both easier to control and easier to get wrong.

### Assumptions and estimates

Below, we describe representative setups we assumed for an ISP and a CSP, and the corresponding estimates for each component.

#### Platform infrastructure (orchestration, APIs, logging, intermediate storage)

**ISP**

- Custom orchestration using Step Functions and Lambda for internal copy jobs.
- Core API endpoints for internal users.
- CloudWatch logging tuned to internal compliance needs.
- Small working buckets for staging and thawing.
- Typical monthly cost: **\$50–150** for these shared services.

**CSP**

- Custom orchestration using Step Functions and Lambda for internal and partner transfers.
- Additional APIs for partner access and federated queries.
- Detailed logging and longer retention for cross‑organisational audit.
- Larger staging and working buckets due to higher partner activity.
- Typical monthly cost: **\$150–350**, reflecting broader use and higher logging needs.



*Note: costs for external orchestration services (e.g. Globus) are not included and should be evaluated separately.*

AWS pricing references:  
[API Gateway](https://aws.amazon.com/api-gateway/pricing/), [Lambda](https://aws.amazon.com/lambda/pricing/), [Step Functions](https://aws.amazon.com/step-functions/pricing/), [CloudWatch](https://aws.amazon.com/cloudwatch/pricing/), [S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/).


#### Internal transfers (same region)

**ISP**

- Datasets accessed by internal projects within the same region.
- Movement is reads from S3 to compute and writes back, with negligible egress cost.
- Occasional intra‑bucket copies and storage class transitions.
- Typical monthly cost: **\$0–20**, primarily S3 request charges.

**CSP**

- Similar internal usage within each account, plus cross‑account movement via bucket policies and VPC endpoints.
- Internal movement costs remain low, but logging and orchestration add small overhead.
- Typical monthly cost: **\$20–50**, including logging and request charges.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/), [EC2 data transfer](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/).

#### Cross‑region transfers

**ISP**

- Occasional replication of key datasets to a secondary region for resilience.
- Typical pattern: TB‑scale per month at most.
- Typical monthly cost: **\$0–50**, depending on replication frequency.

**CSP**

- Routine replication of reference datasets (genome builds, annotations) to partner regions.
- Project outputs replicated on demand when partners request them.
- Movement may be tens of GB to a few TB per month across regions.
- Typical monthly cost: **\$100–300**, depending on the volume of cross‑region transfers.

AWS pricing references:  
[S3 replication and multi‑region storage](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/).

#### External egress (internet, on‑premises, other clouds)

**ISP**

- Internal sharing with minimal external egress; analysts work within the platform.
- Occasional external downloads of smaller datasets for local work.
- Typical monthly cost: **\$0–50** for external egress.

**CSP**

- Regular downloads by partners to on‑premises systems or other clouds.
- Egress to the internet or via Direct Connect becomes a significant line item.
- Partners may download 100 GB to 1 TB per month depending on project activity.
- Typical monthly cost: **\$200–500**, depending on partner behaviour and dataset sizes.

AWS pricing references:  
[EC2 / S3 data transfer out](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/), [Direct Connect](https://aws.amazon.com/directconnect/pricing/).
