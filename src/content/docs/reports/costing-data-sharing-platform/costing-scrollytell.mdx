Running a data‑sharing platform in the cloud involves many cost components, and understanding them is essential for sustainability, funding conversations, and architectural decisions. This report breaks down the costs of operating a genomics data‑sharing platform on AWS, focusing on the period **after data analysis is complete**—when the platform's main job is to store, share, and manage analysis‑ready results.

We do not cover the costs of raw data ingestion, sequencing, or analysis compute, as these are typically managed separately. Instead, we focus on the **platform infrastructure, storage, data movement, and lifecycle management** that keep analysis‑ready data accessible and shareable over time.

The costs vary significantly depending on how the platform is designed and used. To illustrate this, we examine two contrasting scenarios: an internal platform used within a single organisation, and a cross‑organisation platform that regularly shares data with external partners. Both have non‑zero standing costs, but they differ in complexity, governance requirements, and total cost of ownership.

## Two scenarios: Internal vs Cross‑Organisation

### Internal Sharing Platform (ISP)

An **Internal Sharing Platform (ISP)** is used within a single organisation by one or more teams. Users are typically employees or collaborators within the same institution, and data sharing happens within a trusted, internal network.

**Characteristics:**
- Users access the platform via internal SSO or organisational identity providers.
- Data is stored in a single AWS region (or a primary region with optional backup).
- Networking is relatively simple: a single VPC, minimal external endpoints, internal-only data movement.
- Audit and compliance requirements are lighter (internal governance standards).
- Some platform components can scale down or be turned off during periods of low activity.

**Example use case**: a research institute maintains a central repository of analysis‑ready genomics datasets. Teams within the institute query and download datasets as needed, but data rarely leaves the organisation.

**Cost profile**: ISPs typically have lower standing costs and more flexible scaling, resulting in a baseline of **~$500–1,100/month** for a minimal platform.

### Cross‑Organisation Sharing Platform (CSP)

A **Cross‑Organisation Sharing Platform (CSP)** regularly shares data with external partner organisations. Partners may be in different AWS accounts, different regions, or entirely outside AWS.

**Characteristics:**
- External partners access the platform via federated identity (e.g. AAF, external IdPs, or cross‑account IAM roles).
- Data may be replicated across regions for resilience and compliance.
- Networking is more complex: external endpoints (PrivateLink, public APIs), cross‑account access, possibly cross‑region replication.
- Audit and compliance requirements are stringent: detailed access logs, immutable audit trails, longer retention periods.
- Most platform components must stay ready 24/7 to serve external partners; scaling down is limited.

**Example use case**: a national genomics data repository shares curated datasets with research institutions across multiple countries. Partners access data via APIs or direct S3 access, and all access is logged for compliance.

**Cost profile**: CSPs typically have higher standing costs due to external infrastructure, federation, and audit requirements, resulting in a baseline of **~$1,700–4,200/month** for a minimal platform.

## Total cost breakdown

The total monthly cost of a data‑sharing platform is the sum of standing costs, storage costs, data movement costs, and lifecycle management costs.

- **Standing costs** are incurred simply by having the platform running, regardless of activity.
- **Storage costs** scale with the volume of data kept and how long it is retained.
- **Data movement costs** scale with how much data is transferred and where it goes (internal vs external).
- **Lifecycle costs** are the long‑term cumulative effect of retention and archiving decisions.

## Data lifecycle cost evolution (7 years)

Monthly cost is only part of the story. Over a 7-year horizon, retention and deletion policy strongly changes total cost of ownership.

The scrolly below compares three lifecycle strategies across all cost components (standing, storage, and data movement):

- **Full retention**: minimal deletion, maximum retention.
- **Balanced retention**: keep valuable curated outputs, archive or delete the rest.
- **Lean retention**: remove temporary and low-value data quickly.
