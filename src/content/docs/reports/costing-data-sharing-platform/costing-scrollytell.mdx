Running a data-sharing platform on cloud infrastructure costs money in ways that aren't always obvious. Beyond the data itself, you pay to keep infrastructure running, secure access working, and datasets available when needed. Understanding these costs is essential for sustainability, funding conversations, and making smart architectural choices.

This report breaks down the costs of operating a genomics data-sharing platform on **AWS** (Amazon Web Services) **after analysis is complete**—focusing on what it takes to store finished datasets, share them with your team or partners, and keep them accessible over time. We don't cover raw sequencing or analysis compute costs, which are typically managed separately. Instead, we focus on the costs of running and maintaining the sharing platform itself: the infrastructure, storage, access, and governance that make data discoverable and shareable.

The total cost depends heavily on how you design your platform and who uses it. To show this, we compare two scenarios: an internal platform used by one organisation, and a shared platform that regularly exchanges data with external partners. Both incur baseline costs just to stay ready, but they differ significantly in complexity, governance needs, and total spending over time.


## Two scenarios: Internal vs Cross-Organisation

We use two scenarios throughout this report as bookends for cost comparison. They're not exhaustive, but they illustrate the key cost drivers and trade-offs you'll face when designing your platform.

### Internal Sharing Platform

An **internal platform** serves a single organisation. Users are a limited number of employees or trusted collaborators within the same institution. Data is managed centrally and rarely leaves the organisation. An internal platform has these characteristics:

- Simpler access control: users authenticate via your organisation's existing systems.
- Centralised data management: typically one primary location, with optional backup for resilience.
- Internal governance: audit and compliance requirements are set by your organisation alone.
- Flexible operations: platform components can scale down during low activity periods.
- Limited external dependencies: no need to manage external partner access or cross-organisation agreements.

Since all data and operations stay within your organisation and under your control, an internal platform can be designed for simplicity and cost efficiency. You make all the decisions about what data to keep, how long to retain it, and who can access it.

### Cross-Organisation Sharing Platform

A **cross-organisation platform** regularly shares data with external partners—other institutions, research groups, or collaborators outside your organisation. Partners may be in different locations, different cloud environments, or entirely outside the cloud.

A cross-organisation platform has these characteristics:

- Complex access control: you must manage multiple external login systems and establish trust relationships with partners.
- Distributed data management: data may be replicated across multiple locations to meet compliance requirements or serve geographically distant partners.
- Stringent governance: audit trails, access logs, and compliance requirements are often dictated by external agreements or regulations.
- Always-on operations: the platform must remain ready 24/7 to serve external partners; scaling down is limited.
- Multi-stakeholder coordination: decisions about data retention, access, and sharing involve multiple organisations with different needs.

Because a cross-organisation platform must serve external partners reliably and meet stricter governance standards, it requires more infrastructure, more careful management, and more operational overhead.

## Three main cost dimensions


We identify three main components that contribute to a sharing platform costs. **Standing costs** are the baseline expenses of keeping your platform ready: networking, security, monitoring, and the core infrastructure that runs whether or not anyone is using it. **Storage costs** account for the month-to-month expenses of keeping data accessible and stored. **Data movement costs** are what you pay to move data—between users, between locations, or to external partners.

Below, we explore how each component contributes to costs for both scenarios: an Internal Sharing Platform (ISP) and a Cross-Organisation Sharing Platform (CSP). The bars show cumulative monthly costs as we move through each dimension, expressed in USD.

## Total cost breakdown

We can break down each cost dimension into its sub-components to see in more detail where the money is going. The balance between these components shifts depending on whether you're running an internal or cross-organisation platform, but we can identify a few common building blocks for each.


**Standing costs** are made up of five building blocks:
- **Networking infrastructure** – the systems that connect your platform and allow access
- **Core storage and metadata systems** – databases that index and track your datasets
- **Identity and security services** – systems that manage who can access what
- **Monitoring and audit systems** – tools that track activity and maintain compliance records
- **Baseline compute capacity** – the servers or processing power that keeps things running

These exist whether or not anyone is using your platform.

**Storage costs** come from five sources:
- **Hot storage** – keeping frequently accessed data fast and ready
- **Cold storage** – archiving older data in cheaper, slower tiers
- **Metadata and indexing** – systems that help users find and understand datasets
- **Logs and audit trails** – records of access and activity for compliance
- **Replication** – keeping copies of data across multiple locations

**Data movement costs** arise from four areas:
- **Orchestration infrastructure** – systems that coordinate data transfers and sharing
- **Internal movement** – moving data within your platform (usually free)
- **Cross-region movement** – moving data between geographic locations
- **External egress** – moving data to external partners or systems outside the cloud


The charts below show how these components combine for each scenario. Hover over any segment to see its exact cost and what percentage it represents. The two scenarios tell very different stories: the Internal platform is dominated by standing costs, while the Cross-Organisation platform spreads costs more evenly across all three dimensions with data movement becoming a major factor. Prices are per month and shown in USD.

## Data lifecycle cost evolution (7 years)

Monthly cost is only part of the story. Over a 7-year horizon, retention and deletion policy strongly changes total cost of ownership.

The scrolly below compares three lifecycle strategies across all cost components (standing, storage, and data movement):

- **Full retention**: minimal deletion, maximum retention.
- **Balanced retention**: keep valuable curated outputs, archive or delete the rest.
- **Lean retention**: remove temporary and low-value data quickly.
